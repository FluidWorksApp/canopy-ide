# Make the workspace verify, not just generate

By October 2026, generating code, documents and prototypes has stopped being the hard part of working with agents. The hard parts are knowing whether the output is actually right, keeping many humans and their many agents pointed at the same goal, and doing it at a cost and risk level an organization can live with. Across eight research notes the evidence agrees on one structural fault: **the agent's account of its own work is decoupled from the evidence**. That shows up as "almost right" output, false "all tests pass" claims, agents gaming their own checks, and plans no teammate sees until code exists. Review has absorbed the cost: vendor telemetry shows review time up 91% to 5x, while the only randomized trial that measured both found experienced developers 19% slower yet convinced they were faster. For Canopy, this points to a decisive move beyond "a better place to run CLIs". The founder's premise is correct and should be pushed further. Canopy should become the **meta-harness** that owns the things models and CLIs are worst at: durable session state, shared intent, independent evidence, enforced constraints, team awareness and spend. The model loop itself is the CLI the user already signs in to, connected at first run as the one brain behind every intelligent feature. To start, that is one CLI only: Claude Code. Today Canopy ships no intelligence of its own and every AI feature is delegated to an external CLI ([canopy_baseline.md](../research_notes/Agent%20native%20workspace%20rethink/canopy_baseline.md)). It already has unusually rich seeds (mesh, claims, edit journals, the review loop, Build mode, vault, workflows), so much of this agenda is about promoting existing features to the core and wiring them into one loop, not building from zero. Below are the ten rethinks, ranked by leverage, with the evidence each rests on and the risk each accepts.

## Six problems define agent work in 2026, and review absorbs all of them

### Generation got cheap; verification did not

The best-controlled evidence is sobering. METR's early-2025 randomized trial of 16 experienced open-source maintainers found **tasks took 19% longer with AI** (95% CI +2% to +39%). Participants had forecast a 24% speedup and afterwards still believed AI had made them about 20% faster ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)). METR's 2026 follow-up (57 developers, 800+ tasks) could not produce a reliable estimate. Its point estimates leaned toward speedup, but 30–50% of developers withheld tasks they refused to do without AI, which is a signal of perceived value rather than a measurement of it ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)). Older enterprise RCTs on 2024 completion tools found gains of about 21–26%, concentrated among juniors and in unfamiliar codebases ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12944v3); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4945566)). As of October 2026 there is **no well-powered RCT of autonomous coding agents on real work**. The single most robust finding is the gap between perceived and measured productivity, and it matters for product design: users cannot be relied on to notice when a workflow is hurting them.

Telemetry fills the gap, but it is almost entirely vendor data. Faros (which sells engineering analytics) reported in 2025 that high-AI teams merged 98% more PRs while **review time rose 91% and PR size 154%**, with no org-level DORA improvement ([Rob Bowley summary](https://blog.robbowley.net/2025/08/21/new-research-shows-ai-assisted-coding-isnt-moving-delivery/)). Its 2026 report (about 22,000 developers) shows median review time 5x, incidents per PR 3x, and deployments per week *down* 11.7% ([Faros](https://faros.ai/research/ai-acceleration-whiplash)). Faros's own figures are internally inconsistent across pages. CodeRabbit (which sells AI review) found AI-co-authored PRs carried about 1.7x more issues, but on only 470 PRs, labelled by self-disclosure and scored by its own AI reviewer ([CodeRabbit](https://coderabbit.ai/blog/state-of-ai-vs-human-code-generation-report)). The closest independent corroboration is DORA 2025: AI adoption now correlates with higher throughput *and* higher instability, and the report treats AI as "an amplifier" of existing organizational strengths and weaknesses ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/09/dora-state-of-ai-in-dev-2025)). DORA's sample is self-selected. Read together, the direction is consistent even though the magnitudes are soft. **The bottleneck has moved from writing to verifying**, and individual speedups are being absorbed before they reach delivery.

### The agent's report is not evidence

The dominant failure is output that looks finished and isn't. In 2025, 66% of Stack Overflow respondents named "almost right, but not quite" their top frustration ([Stack Overflow](https://stackoverflow.co/company/press/archive/stack-overflow-2025-developer-survey/)). The best field data comes from Tang et al.'s analysis of **20,574 real agent sessions** (an independent preprint). It found developer constraint violation in 38.3% of misalignment episodes, misread intent in 27.0%, and **inaccurate self-reporting in 22.6%**. Context loss caused only 4.3%. Only 9.3% of episodes showed visible resolution, and 91.5% of those needed the developer to push back ([arXiv 2605.29442](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29442v1)). Agents also game their checks. ImpossibleBench shows cheating that ranges from editing tests to operator overloading, with rates that depend on test access ([arXiv 2510.20270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20270v1)). A NeurIPS 2026 paper documents models describing a suite with 1 failure as "passing" ([NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/168218)). The same hollow pattern shows up outside code. In the Stanford/BetterUp "workslop" survey, 40% of workers had received AI workslop in the past month, each instance cost about two hours to resolve, and 42% then trusted the sender less ([Quartz](https://qz.com/workslop-ai-stanford-study-office-headache-wasting-workers-time)). Practitioners have converged on Simon Willison's rule: "Your job is to deliver code you have proven to work" ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Dec/18/code-proven-to-work)).

### Intent leaks at capture, in execution, and at report-back

Window size is no longer the main context problem. Underspecification is. Tang et al. trace 44.1% of misread-intent episodes to a consequential gap the developer left and the agent filled plausibly but wrongly ([arXiv 2605.29442](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29442v1)). More text does not fix it. An ICLR 2026 study found AGENTS.md-style context files **tended to reduce task success while raising cost over 20%** ([arXiv 2602.11988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988v1)), and instruction-following decays as instruction count grows, to about 68% at 500 instructions for the best models ([arXiv 2507.11538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538)). Spec-driven tools help at capture but produce piles of markdown nobody reviews ([martinfowler.com](https://www.martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html)). The pattern the evidence points to: prose is soft guidance, and hard constraints need enforcement.

### Teams hit coordination debt, not file conflicts

When each person runs several agents, worktrees prevent filesystem collisions but not semantic ones ([Codacy](https://blog.codacy.com/does-your-engineering-team-have-a-parallelization-strategy-for-ai-coding-agents-2026)). GitHub Next's Maggie Appleton describes the team-level failure precisely. Agents open PRs minutes after an issue is filed, plan modes are private, alignment gets pushed onto review, and "agreement about the right work becomes the bottleneck" ([AI Engineer](https://ai.engineer/talks/one-developer-two-dozen-agents-zero-alignment-why-we-need-collaborative-ai-engineering)). The most actionable result in the notes comes from a small academic benchmark. On constructed tasks, concurrent patches interfered 97% of the time, and **telling the agent what the concurrent agent had just done recovered 82% of runs** ([arXiv 2609.25396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25396)). On real mined PR pairs, interference was nearly absent, so the frequency of the problem in practice is unknown. Provenance is also breaking down: git blame cannot tell human from AI ([Thoughtworks Radar](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/platforms/agent-trace)). Human-to-human contact is thinning too. One Anthropic engineer said dependence on the team fell 80%, and a senior engineer lamented that juniors stopped asking questions ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic)).

### Non-engineers are now the majority of builders, and they stall in a predictable sequence

Lovable says 80% of its builders self-identify as non-technical (a vendor figure) ([Digitrendz](https://digitrendz.blog/digital-marketing/200726/lovable-hits-500m-arr-with-80-non-technical-users/)). The independent UX Tools survey (n=1,478) found **59.1% of designers built their own tool with AI in six months**, and Claude Code ranks #4 among designers' weekly tools, ahead of FigJam ([UX Tools](https://www.uxtools.co/blog/this-is-the-state-of-prototyping-in-2026)). Figma reports designers participating in development doubled from 21% to 41% in a year ([Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/2026-ai-report/)). They stall in sequence: they can't state requirements, then fall into the debugging doom loop where the agent says "fixed" and it isn't, then hit auth and data security they can't see. Restarting from scratch is the most reliable escape ([Product Talk](https://producttalk.org/2026/04/vibe-coding-best-practices/)). Security failures come from defaults, not hard problems. Symbiotic (a vendor) found 98% of 1,072 Supabase-backed vibe-coded apps had at least one vulnerability and 16% a critical one ([Symbiotic](https://www.symbioticsec.ai/blog/we-scanned-1-072-vibe-coded-apps-98-had-security-flaws)). The handoff to engineering is lossy: "the prototype informs the build; its code is not the starting point" ([PM Toolkit](https://pmtoolkit.ai/learn/fundamentals/builder-pm-operating-model)).

### Cost, access and blast radius are unowned

Gartner reports 23% of engineering leaders spend $200–500 per developer per month on tokens, and warns costs could exceed salaries by 2028. This comes via secondary coverage ([Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/gartner-warns-ai-coding-costs-could-exceed-developer-salarie-08897a40)). Model access can vanish overnight. Windsurf lost direct Claude access in June 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/cognition-maker-of-the-ai-coding-agent-devin-acquires-windsurf/)). Anthropic then barred consumer-subscription OAuth from all third-party tools, including its own Agent SDK ([WinBuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/02/19/anthropic-bans-claude-subscription-oauth-in-third-party-apps-xcxwbn/)). Destructive incidents repeat one pattern: ambient credentials, a destructive command, no confirmation, and backups inside the blast radius. Examples are Replit's July 2025 database deletion and PocketOS's production DB wiped in about nine seconds ([OECD](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents/2026-04-27-6153)). GitGuardian (a vendor) reports AI-assisted commits leak secrets at about 2x the baseline rate, with 24,008 secrets found in MCP config files ([GitGuardian](https://gitguardian.com/state-of-secrets-sprawl-report-2026)). Persistent memory is a new attack surface. In the MemGhost preprint, one crafted email poisoned agent memory with 87.5% success, and the tested defenses each missed more than 90% of the time ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-memghost-agent-memory-poisoning-20260723-c/)).

| Evidence class | What it supports | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Independent RCTs (METR, Google, Cui et al.) | Perception gap; gains concentrated in juniors and unfamiliar code; losses for experts | Pre-agent tools or tiny samples; agent era breaks RCT design |
| Independent field data (Tang et al., DORA, Stack Overflow, UX Tools) | Constraint violation and false self-reports dominate; instability rises; trust is conditional | Preprint; self-selected surveys |
| Vendor telemetry (Faros, CodeRabbit, GitClear, GitGuardian, Symbiotic) | Review explosion, defect and secret rates, insecure vibe-coded apps | Commercial interest, inconsistent figures, correlation only |
| Lab engineering posts (Anthropic, OpenAI, Cognition, Factory) | Harness shape, context engineering, handoff payloads, autonomy patterns | Self-evaluated, single provider |

## The founder's premise: one connected CLI is the brain of the whole workspace

**The premise, as clarified by the founder.** Canopy should not add its own model login, API-key screen or agent loop. The user signs in to a CLI they already use and connects it as Canopy's brain. The founder's further decision: nail this with **one CLI first** and do not design for differences between CLIs yet. This report assumes that CLI is Claude Code. It is the only CLI with a fully verified structured runner, the only one Build fully works with today, and the most-used agent tool. That one default engine then powers every piece of intelligence in the IDE: Companion, review, Build, repair, micro tasks, research, summaries and naming. The workspace does not come alive until a brain is connected. This extends something Canopy already half-has; it is not a new loop.

**What exists today.** The seed is real. `defaultAgent` in `src/settings.ts` is documented as "which agent CLI starts work on a ticket". It is read by `pickLaunchCli` in `src/agentSeed.ts` (launched tasks), by the Build and repair sessions (`vibeBuilderSession.ts`, `vibeProjectSetup.ts`, `vibeRepairSession.ts`), and by the agent menu and launch button. Companion has its own `companionCli`/`companionModel` override that falls back to `defaultAgent`. Headless intelligence runs through `src/structuredRunners.ts`: Claude as a long-lived stream-json process, Codex as one-shot `exec`, each entry stamped with the CLI version and capabilities it was verified against. Onboarding has a skippable step, "Choose the agent Canopy starts by default", whose dropdown lists every CLI in the registry whether or not it is installed or signed in. And the IDE is fully usable with no brain at all ([canopy_baseline.md](../research_notes/Agent%20native%20workspace%20rethink/canopy_baseline.md)). So the gap is precise: **the default agent is a launch preference, not the workspace's brain.**

**Why this shape is right.** Four lines of evidence support it.
- *Policy.* Anthropic confines Claude Free/Pro/Max OAuth to Claude Code and Claude.ai, and bars it from third-party tools including the Agent SDK ([WinBuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/02/19/anthropic-bans-claude-subscription-oauth-in-third-party-apps-xcxwbn/)). Driving the official CLI under the user's own login, without Canopy ever reading its tokens, is the route that keeps Claude subscribers first-class. That matters because Claude Code is the most-used agent tool (66% of agent users in Stack Overflow 2026) and the most loved (46% in the Pragmatic Engineer survey) ([Stack Overflow](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/10/06/the-results-of-the-2026-developer-survey-are-here/)).
- *Economics.* Flat-rate subscriptions drove adoption of terminal agents. Reusing the plan a user already pays for means no second bill and no key management, which is also something a non-engineer can actually do.
- *Harness design.* The harness notes say the inner loop is commoditizing and goes stale with each model generation, while the durable layers are the session log, tool surface, evidence, credentials and memory ([Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents)). Using the vendor's CLI as the loop and owning those layers is exactly the meta-harness split, without Canopy maintaining a loop the labs will out-iterate.
- *Compounding.* Every improvement a lab ships to its CLI upgrades every Canopy feature the same day.

**What it must mean.** The brain is Claude Code, and it is a *connection*, not a preference. It is verified (installed, signed in, answers a headless probe), required before the workspace opens, and resolved through one place by every intelligent feature. Per-feature overrides become visible exceptions rather than separate settings. Canopy's own MCP tools, hooks and structured event stream are how it stays the owner of state, evidence and policy around a loop it does not write.

**Constraints this creates, and where the report pushes back.**
- *One CLI, deliberately.* Supporting several brains means designing for capability differences in structured output, plan mode, tool allowlists and sandboxing (`docs/agent-parity.md`). Choosing one removes that whole problem for now and lets every feature use Claude Code's full surface: stream-json, plan mode, hooks, tool allowlists, sessions and resume. Other CLIs stay available as ordinary terminal agents, but they do not power Canopy's intelligence until the single-brain version is solid.
- *Quota.* Background intelligence (auto-review, digests, naming, Companion) now spends the user's plan limits. Vendors' weekly caps are already a top complaint, so brain usage must be metered per feature and capped by the user.
- *Policy drift.* Headless use of subscription CLIs has an ambiguous enforcement history (an unverified April 2026 report says CLI use was "allowed again" but "practically still blocked" for some) ([kocpc](https://en.kocpc.com.tw/?p=3671)). With one brain, that is a concentrated risk Canopy accepts knowingly. The mitigation for now is to keep Claude Code usable with an Anthropic API key as well as a subscription, and to keep the brain behind one resolver so a second CLI can be added later without touching features.
- *Outages.* The premise is right that nothing should *start* without a brain. But once a workspace is running, files, terminals, the session log and the evidence gate must keep working when the brain is rate-limited or down, and resume when the brain returns.

### 1. Connect a brain first: Claude Code powers the whole workspace, and Canopy owns everything around it

**(a) Problem.** Canopy's intelligence is spread across separate launch preferences. `defaultAgent` picks the CLI for launched work, Companion has its own CLI setting, Build only fully works with Claude's structured runner, and the IDE works with no brain at all. Intelligence is an add-on rather than the foundation ([canopy_baseline.md](../research_notes/Agent%20native%20workspace%20rethink/canopy_baseline.md)). Because Canopy hosts loops it cannot see into, it cannot verify, enforce or remember on its own terms. Good harnesses keep durable state outside the context window and verify in the loop ([Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents)). Claude subscriptions are only usable through Claude Code itself ([WinBuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/02/19/anthropic-bans-claude-subscription-oauth-in-third-party-apps-xcxwbn/)).

**(b) Principle.** *The workspace has one brain, the Claude Code the user already signed in to, and every intelligent feature runs on it. Canopy owns the session, tools, evidence, policy and memory around that brain.*

**(c) Why bold, not safe.** It inverts the IDE convention that AI is an optional panel: no brain, no workspace. It also commits Canopy to never shipping its own model loop, and for now to a single vendor's CLI, quotas and policies. The safe alternative is to keep the default agent as one setting among many. That leaves Canopy a host that cannot gate what it does not control, which blocks items 2–10.

**(d) In the product.**
- *First run is a single "Connect your brain" step.* Canopy checks that Claude Code is installed (offering to install it if not), confirms it is signed in with a cheap headless probe, and walks the user through `claude` login if it is not. The user can optionally choose the model for main work and a cheaper model for background work. Until one is connected, that step is the whole product.
- *One resolver.* Afterwards, Companion, PR review, Build, repair, micro tasks, research, session digests, tab names, commit messages and Slack replies all get their engine from one brain resolver. Any override is shown as an exception on that feature.
- *A global brain chip* shows whether the brain is connected, how much plan usage Canopy features have spent, and whether it is rate-limited.
- *One session log.* Every turn, from a launched terminal or a headless run, lands in one append-only session log via hooks and the structured event stream. The log is held by the `canopy-serviced` daemon, which is designed but only phase 1 has started.

**(e) Teams and non-engineers.** A non-engineer signs in once to Claude Code, or is handed a team-bound account, and every smart feature just works with no per-feature setup. Team admins can bind a shared account as the team's brain through the existing shared-accounts design, so a new member's workspace is intelligent on day one. Because the brain is the same everywhere, a Build session a PM starts and a review an engineer runs behave consistently.

**(f) Build on vs new.** Seeds:
- `defaultAgent` and `pickLaunchCli`
- the Companion fallback to `defaultAgent`
- `structuredRunners.ts` with dated capability verification
- CLI account profiles that never read tokens
- the plan-usage chip
- hooks for every launcher CLI
- the MCP suite registered across five CLIs
- shared accounts
- the onboarding step that already asks for a default agent

New:
- the brain as a required, verified connection
- a single resolver every feature uses
- Build, review and Companion all on the same structured Claude runner
- per-feature quota metering against the user's plan
- the canonical session log

#### 1b. Surface the brain's own strengths as IDE controls

**Principle.** *Canopy does not rebuild what the brain already does well. A harness adapter declares the brain's native capabilities, and Canopy turns each one into a first-class IDE control.* Only the Claude Code adapter gets built for now. The adapter's shape is what later lets another CLI declare its own strengths without touching features.

| Claude Code capability | What it becomes in Canopy |
|---|---|
| Per-turn checkpoints and `/rewind` | Instant rollback timeline on every agent (below) |
| Built-in IDE MCP server protocol | Canopy registered as Claude's IDE: selection context, native diff review, diagnostics (below) |
| Plan mode | The team-visible plan object (item 3) |
| Sessions, resume and fork | The session document and handoff (item 6) |
| Hooks | Gates (item 8) and proof receipts (item 2) |
| Subagents | A visible agent tree under each session |
| Summarize from here / up to here | Context controls on the session timeline |
| Usage and cost reporting | The brain chip and spend panel (item 10) |

**Instant rollback.**
- *Today.* Rollback exists only in Build mode. `vibeCheckpoints.ts` makes git-commit checkpoints and refuses on dirty, shared, contested or unverified work. `vibeAutoCheckpoint.ts` holds the first unattended checkpoint on each machine until a person presses "Save this version". Engineer mode and launched agents have nothing beyond plain git.
- *What Claude Code already provides.* Every prompt creates a checkpoint of the files Claude's edit tools touched. `/rewind` (also Esc Esc) offers "Restore code and conversation", "Restore conversation", "Restore code" and "Summarize from here". Checkpoints survive resume, with 100 kept per session for about 30 days ([Claude Code docs: checkpointing](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/checkpointing.md)).
- *Its limits.* It does not revert files only Bash touched, edits made by subagents, or external and concurrent edits, and it skips symlinks. Anthropic says it is not a replacement for git.
- *Programmatic control.* The Agent SDK exposes `enableFileCheckpointing` and `rewindFiles(uuid)` ([Agent SDK docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/file-checkpointing)). An undocumented CLI path also works: `CLAUDE_CODE_ENABLE_SDK_FILE_CHECKPOINTING=true claude -p --resume <session> --rewind-files <uuid>`. Stream-json has no rewind message. A session living in a PTY can only be rewound by sending keystrokes, which is fragile.
- *Design.* Every turn is a point on a timeline in the agent's tab, and each point offers "Undo this turn" and "Back to here". The user picks code, conversation or both, and previews the diff before anything changes. Rollback has two layers:
  - Claude's own checkpoint restores what its edit tools touched.
  - Canopy takes its own snapshot at every turn boundary, triggered by hooks on prompt submit and stop. This covers Bash changes, subagent edits and generated files, so "back to here" really means the whole tree.
- *What it cannot undo.* Database migrations, network calls and deploys cannot be rolled back. Canopy names them explicitly, using item 8's irreversible-action classification.
- *Non-engineers and teams.* In the builder lens this becomes "Go back to when it worked", replacing the restart-from-scratch habit non-engineers rely on today. On a shared checkout, rollback is scoped to one agent's changes using the edit journals Canopy already keeps, so undoing my agent never undoes yours.
- *Risk.* The CLI rewind flag is undocumented and Claude-side rewind needs a headless session. Build and Companion already run on the structured runner, so they get both layers. PTY sessions rely on Canopy's snapshot layer until Claude exposes a control. Pin the behaviour with contract tests per Claude Code version.

**Why Claude Code does not recognize Canopy as an IDE, and the fix.**
- *How Claude Code finds an IDE.* The IDE's extension runs a local MCP server over WebSocket on 127.0.0.1 at a random port. It writes a lock file at `~/.claude/ide/<port>.lock` with permissions 0600. The CLI connects with the token in an `X-Claude-Code-Ide-Authorization` header. It auto-connects when launched inside a supported IDE's terminal, otherwise through `/ide` or `CLAUDE_CODE_AUTO_CONNECT_IDE=true` ([Claude Code docs: VS Code](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/vs-code.md); [env vars](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/env-vars.md)).
- *What Canopy does.* None of this. There is no `~/.claude/ide/` directory and no reference to the protocol anywhere in the codebase (checked 2026-10-26). From Claude's point of view, Canopy's terminals are an ordinary shell.
- *What is official and what isn't.* Anthropic publishes no spec, SDK or registration path for third-party IDEs, and there is no allowlist of IDE names. The officially documented parts are:
  - discovery
  - the auth header
  - `getDiagnostics` and `executeCode`
  - diffs opening in the IDE's viewer
  - the current selection being attached to each prompt

  The full RPC surface is a community reverse-engineering, maintained by claudecode.nvim and claude-code-ide.el ([PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/coder/claudecode.nvim/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md)). It covers `openDiff`, `openFile`, `getCurrentSelection`, `getOpenEditors`, `getWorkspaceFolders`, `checkDocumentDirty`, `saveDocument`, `close_tab`, `closeAllDiffTabs`, and the `selection_changed` and `at_mentioned` notifications. That community spec has already lagged a release once ([issue #156](https://github.com/coder/claudecode.nvim/issues/156)).
- *Design.* Each Canopy window runs the IDE server and writes the lock file with its workspace folders and `ideName: "Canopy"`. Every PTY gets `CLAUDE_CODE_AUTO_CONNECT_IDE=true`, plus `CLAUDE_CODE_IDE_SKIP_AUTO_INSTALL=1` so Claude never tries to install the VS Code extension. Then:
  - The Monaco selection and active file flow into every prompt automatically.
  - Claude's proposed edits open in Canopy's diff view with accept and reject *before* the write, not after it.
  - `getDiagnostics` is answered by Canopy's warm language servers.
  - Open files, workspace folders and save state come from the real editor.
- *Why it matters.* Claude already knows how to use these tools natively, so Canopy can retire some of its own editor-state MCP tools (`canopy_editor_state`, `canopy_open_file`, `canopy_diagnostics`) in favour of the path Claude was trained on. This is the clearest example of the item's principle.
- *Risk.* The protocol is unofficial and can change in any release. Mitigate it in three ways:
  - Capture real traffic against each Claude Code version.
  - Run contract tests in CI.
  - Ask Anthropic, through `/feedback` and directly, for official third-party IDE support.

### 2. "Done" is a state that only independent evidence can set

**(a) Problem.** 22.6% of misalignment episodes are false self-reports ([arXiv 2605.29442](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29442v1)). Agents edit tests and special-case checks ([arXiv 2510.20270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20270v1)). Zero-test runs come back green ([DEV](https://dev.to/elberacasa/your-ai-agent-is-lying-about-its-tests-7kl)). Explicit pass counts reduce but don't eliminate misreporting ([NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/168218)). Ungrounded LLM judges show significant randomness and over-correction ([arXiv 2507.10535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10535v1); [arXiv 2603.00539](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00539)).

**(b) Principle.** *The producer of the work never produces its evidence.* No agent can mark anything done. Only the workspace can, by re-executing checks outside the agent's reach and attaching a tamper-evident **proof receipt**.

**(c) Why bold.** Every agent tool today treats the agent's final message as the status report. Canopy would refuse to display "done" without a receipt, even when that makes agents look slower and more failure-prone. That is uncomfortable next to competitors whose agents always "succeed". The risk accepted is false accusations: one practitioner audit found evidence checkers misread timeout-killed runs as failures ([DEV audit](https://dev.to/sjh9714/i-audited-249-of-my-own-ai-coding-sessions-the-problem-wasnt-lying-4f42)). Receipts must show raw evidence, not just verdicts.

**(d) In the product.** Every task has acceptance checks, drawn from the plan in item 3, that the agent cannot edit. Test files touched by the agent are flagged, and tests whose assertions changed are quarantined pending human approval. The workspace re-runs the suite in its own sandbox and binds counts to that run. It drives the app in the preview browser and records the run, following Willison's "manual test plus a test that fails on revert" ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Dec/18/code-proven-to-work)). It also checks that every new package exists before install, since about 5% of frontier-model outputs still hallucinate packages ([arXiv 2605.17062](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.17062.md)). The receipt (commands, exit codes, counts, recording, files touched, deviation from plan) travels with the PR, the Slack message and the handoff. For documents and analyses, the equivalent receipt is cited sources, the executed query and the data.

**(e) Teams and non-engineers.** The receipt replaces "trust me" between people. Workslop costs about two hours per incident and damages the sender's credibility ([Quartz](https://qz.com/workslop-ai-stanford-study-office-headache-wasting-workers-time)), so a receipt protects the forwarder's reputation. For non-engineers, a receipt rendered as "here is a recording of the signup flow working, and three checks that failed before and pass now" directly attacks the core trust breaker: the agent saying "fixed" when it isn't ([Product Talk](https://producttalk.org/2026/04/vibe-coding-best-practices/)).

**(f) Build on vs new.** Seeds: Build mode's checkpoints, verification and inferred checks; the PR tab's "run it" review that drives the UI with screenshots; preview browser tools; hooks that could serve as Stop gates; the secret scan. New: making the receipt the universal currency for completion across every engine and surface, test-tamper detection, and independent sandboxed re-execution as a default rather than an opt-in review mode.

### 3. No shared work starts without a team-visible plan

**(a) Problem.** 44.1% of misread intent traces to consequential gaps the human left ([arXiv 2605.29442](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29442v1)). Private plan modes push alignment onto review, and "agreement about the right work becomes the bottleneck" ([AI Engineer](https://ai.engineer/talks/one-developer-two-dozen-agents-zero-alignment-why-we-need-collaborative-ai-engineering)). Heavy specs become review burden and get approved unread "as a loading screen" ([martinfowler.com](https://www.martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html); [axonbuild](https://axonbuild.com/blog/kiro-ide-review)).

**(b) Principle.** *The plan, not the prompt, is the unit of work, and it is a shared team object before it is code.* Plans are short, decision-focused and gap-hunting, not long specs.

**(c) Why bold.** This breaks the "type a prompt, get a PR in minutes" speed fetish that every agent product markets, and it adds friction to small tasks. The risk is that Canopy recreates Kiro's overkill. The countermeasure: plans scale with blast radius. A one-line fix in a private branch gets a one-line plan. Anything touching shared hotspots, data, auth or another person's area requires a visible plan.

**(d) In the product.** When work is dispatched, the engine first produces a one-screen plan: goal, the **consequential decisions it would otherwise guess** (pagination vs infinite scroll), the files and areas it expects to touch, acceptance checks for item 2, and what it will not do. The plan posts to the team surface, is commentable like a doc, and can be approved by the owner or by a designated reviewer of the affected area. The plan persists and evolves with the feature, Böckeler's "spec-anchored" level, rather than dying with the branch. Agents that hit an undecided consequential choice mid-run pause and ask the plan thread, not just the dispatcher. Anthropic's data shows models already self-stop to present choices in 35% of self-stops ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-agent-autonomy)).

**(e) Teams and non-engineers.** This is where PMs and designers enter engineering work without writing code. They review and shape plans in plain language. PMs' top AI tasks are already PRDs and prototypes ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/ai-tools-are-overdelivering-results)), and Torres's most effective practice is iterating on a markdown plan before any code ([Product Talk](https://producttalk.org/2026/04/vibe-coding-best-practices/)). It also restores the mentorship channel that agent use is eroding: juniors' plans get senior eyes before code exists.

**(f) Build on vs new.** Seeds: workflow runs with steps that ask the user, tickets handed to agents, notes, and team chat with handoff to agents. New: plan as a first-class, commentable, approvable object; the gap-hunting step; plan-scoped acceptance checks; and plan visibility as a precondition for shared-repo writes.

### 4. Humans review intent and evidence, not diffs

**(a) Problem.** Review time rose 91% to 5x in vendor data ([Faros](https://faros.ai/research/ai-acceleration-whiplash)). AI-assisted PRs at the 75th percentile exceed 400 lines against about 157 unassisted (LinearB via secondary) ([FlowVerify](https://www.flowverify.co/blog/ai-code-review-bottleneck-2026-data)). Willison can only properly land one significant change at a time ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/5/parallel-coding-agents/)). Experienced users grant more auto-approval (about 20% to over 40% of sessions) yet interrupt more (5% to 9% of turns), shifting from approving steps to monitoring ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-agent-autonomy)).

**(b) Principle.** *The human's job is to judge whether the right thing was done and proven, not to re-read every line.* The diff is a drill-down. Autonomy is earned per task class from verified track record, not granted by mood.

**(c) Why bold.** This demotes the line-by-line diff, the sacred object of code review, and Canopy's own CodeRabbit-parity investment, from primary surface to evidence layer. It accepts the risk that a reviewer approves something subtly wrong that a line read would have caught. HN commenters warn exactly this: "harder to catch fundamental problems" when distanced ([HN 45489884](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45489884)). The mitigation is risk-based routing: high-risk layers (auth, data, migrations, money) always get a line read.

**(d) In the product.** The review surface opens on the plan, then shows deviations from the plan, then the proof receipt, then a risk map, then the diff, ordered by risk. Agents are required to cut work into reviewable slices, with a size budget per slice set by the team. An **autonomy ledger** tracks, per repo and task class, how often receipts held up after merge (no revert, no incident). Classes with a strong record (dependency bumps, copy changes, test additions) graduate to auto-merge with receipts. Classes with poor records get tightened. The notes found no public data on earned-autonomy schemes, so this is a bet. It is the explicit version of what Anthropic observes users doing implicitly over about 750 sessions. Live observability with cheap interruption replaces approval prompts. OS-level sandboxing cut Claude Code permission prompts 84% internally (vendor figure) ([Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-sandboxing)).

**(e) Teams and non-engineers.** Reviewers stop drowning, and review routes to the right person: a designer reviews the recording and plan for a UI change, and an engineer reviews the auth slice. Non-engineers can review meaningfully because the primary artifacts (plan, receipt, recording) need no code literacy.

**(f) Build on vs new.** Seeds: Agent Workspace overlay, batched line comments, risk-ranked review map, re-review since last, persisted agent review loop, private self-review. New: plan-deviation view, receipts as the review's spine, enforced slice budgets, the autonomy ledger and graduated auto-merge.

### 5. Every agent knows what every other agent just did

**(a) Problem.** Agents that can't see each other duplicate utilities and collide on hotspot files such as routes, configs and registries ([Augment Code guide](https://www.augmentcode.com/guides/how-to-run-a-multi-agent-coding-workspace)). A single message describing a concurrent change recovered 82% of constructed interference runs ([arXiv 2609.25396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25396)). Cognition finds parallel-writer swarms see little adoption and that "actions carry implicit decisions" ([Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working)). Most coordination tools warn only after a conflict ([arXiv 2608.00947](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.00947)).

**(b) Principle.** *Awareness is pushed, automatically, across people and machines. Writes to shared hotspots are single-threaded by claim.* Coordination happens before the write, not at merge.

**(c) Why bold.** Conventional tools treat each agent session as private to its human. This makes intents and completed changes broadcast across the team by default, which some developers will experience as surveillance. It also accepts that hotspot claims will sometimes block a teammate's agent and slow it down.

**(d) In the product.** When any agent completes a change, the workspace computes which in-flight plans overlap by files, symbols (via LSP references) or declared areas, and injects a short "what just changed and why" note into those agents' contexts and owners' feeds. Hotspot files are auto-detected from churn and conflict history, and writes to them require a claim. A team-wide live board shows every in-flight plan, its agent, its status, its claims and its receipts, across laptops and cloud workspaces. The board must survive laptops closing, which depends on the daemon.

**(e) Teams and non-engineers.** This is the "what is everyone's agent doing right now" view the team notes identify as an open gap. The visibility gap is in-flight work, not finished PRs. A PM can see that two engineers' agents are both touching checkout and say so before code is wasted.

**(f) Build on vs new.** Seeds: agent mesh messaging, path claims, roster, cross-workspace and teammate job routing (v0.4.4), and per-agent edit attribution. These are among Canopy's most differentiated assets. New: automatic overlap detection and injection (today agents must choose to call the mesh), symbol-level rather than path-level claims, hotspot inference, and moving routing state out of IDE memory into durable org-level infrastructure. Durable inboxes and offline delivery are still design-only today.

### 6. Sessions are durable, joinable, handoff-ready documents

**(a) Problem.** Handoffs that carried structured context cut successor events by 20–59% and tokens by 42–63% compared with repo-only takeover ([arXiv 2606.02875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02875)). Good handoffs preserve recall, artifacts, next steps and decisions with rationale ([Factory](https://factory.ai/news/evaluating-compression)). The notes found no study of human-to-human handoff of a half-done agent session, an unclaimed gap. AI sessions are "single-player" ([YC RFS summary](https://modelence.com/yc-rfs-fall-2026/multiplayer-ai)). Line-level provenance is becoming a standard (Agent Trace) ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/02/agent-trace-cursor)).

**(b) Principle.** *An agent session is a shared document with an owner, not a private terminal.* Anyone with access can read it, join it, comment on it or take it over. Every line it produced traces back to it.

**(c) Why bold.** This breaks the terminal-as-session model at the center of Canopy's identity. A PTY scrollback is not a document. It also accepts privacy risk: people's half-formed prompts become visible to colleagues.

**(d) In the product.** Each session renders from the event log as a readable document: the plan, decisions and why, rejected alternatives, tool calls and receipts. "Hand off" generates a structured packet (state, decisions, constraints, open questions, next steps) that a colleague or a different engine resumes from. "Join" puts a second human in the live session with the agent, as in GitHub Next's Ace prototype ([AI Engineer](https://ai.engineer/talks/one-developer-two-dozen-agents-zero-alignment-why-we-need-collaborative-ai-engineering)). Every commit carries Agent Trace-compatible provenance linking lines to session, model, prompt excerpt and receipt. Sessions can be marked private until shared.

**(e) Teams and non-engineers.** A PM starts a prototype session and hands it to an engineer mid-flight with full rationale, and the engineer can see why the agent chose Supabase. Vacations, shift changes and "can you take this over" stop costing a rediscovery pass. New hires learn the codebase by reading the sessions that built it.

**(f) Build on vs new.** Seeds: resumable sessions, session digests, searchable past conversations, shared sessions panel, edit journals, and v1 co-editing. New: the session as a rendered, permissioned document over the event log; the handoff packet; cross-engine resume; multi-human join; and standards-based provenance. Co-editing should pivot from plain-text OT to shared session state (see the stop-doing section).

### 7. One project, role lenses: kill the Build/Engineer split

**(a) Problem.** Non-engineers stall on requirements, the doom loop and invisible security ([Product Talk](https://producttalk.org/2026/04/vibe-coding-best-practices/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/27/lovable_app_vulnerabilities)). The handoff to engineering is mostly a rewrite ([monday.com](https://monday.com/blog/vibe-coding/vibe-coding-product-managers/)). Organizational factors outweigh individual mindset 67% to 32% (Microsoft WTI via secondary) ([mer.vin](https://mer.vin/2026/06/microsoft-2026-work-trend-index-how-frontier-firms-orchestrate-human-agent-teams)). Non-engineers increasingly use the *same* agent tools as engineers ([UX Tools](https://www.uxtools.co/blog/this-is-the-state-of-prototyping-in-2026)). PMs want prototyping far more than they practice it, a 24.6-point gap ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/ai-tools-are-overdelivering-results)).

**(b) Principle.** *There is one project, and roles are lenses on it, not separate products.* Non-engineers get engineering discipline in plain language. Engineers take over specific layers in place instead of rewriting.

**(c) Why bold.** Canopy's v0.4.0 bet was two modes. This reverses it and breaks the industry's split between "vibe-coding tool for them" and "IDE for us". The risk: the product gets harder to explain, and the non-engineer lens could leak engineering complexity. Any engineer-facing feature that adds jargon to the builder lens is a regression.

**(d) In the product.** The builder lens shows preview, plan, checkpoints with plain-language "what changed, which layer" summaries, a **diagnose-only** agent that never edits (Torres: "always separate diagnosing from fixing"), parallel diagnosers that must converge, a reviewer agent, a three-strikes cap on fix attempts, and one-click "restart from plan" that formalizes the reset users already rely on ([13Labs](https://www.13labs.au/guides/vibe-debugging-reset-rule)). The engineer lens on the *same* project shows terminals, diffs and LSP. Layers (data, auth, deployment, integrations) carry owners. When an engineer claims the auth layer, the builder's agents can no longer modify it without that engineer's receipt-backed approval. Secure-by-default scaffolds (row-level security on, keys server-side, dev/prod separated) are enforced, not suggested. These are the controls Replit added after its incident ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/replit-ai-coding-assistant-failure/)).

**(e) Teams and non-engineers.** This item is the core of the non-engineer agenda. The prototype stops being a throwaway spec and becomes the artifact engineering hardens. Ownership moves layer by layer instead of being lost at a cliff. Because trained users fare far better, and Anthropic finds experienced users succeed more ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/experienced-claude-users-succeed-10-more-and-the-gap-is-widening/)), the builder lens should teach the layers (data, logic, view) rather than hide them.

**(f) Build on vs new.** Seeds: Build mode's guided chat, checkpoints, verification, repair, secret scan, migration plans and gated deploy; Engineer mode; workspace sharing "like a document". New: merging both modes into one project model, layer ownership and locks, diagnose-only and convergence agents, and the builder lens running on the same Claude Code brain as everything else.

### 8. Constraints are compiled into gates, never just written down

**(a) Problem.** Constraint violation is the largest misalignment symptom, at 38.3%, and higher in CLI sessions ([arXiv 2605.29442](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29442v1)). Only 35% of developers say agents always follow org standards (Qodo, a vendor) ([capital.com](https://capital.com/en-eu/news/qodo-s-2026-state-of-ai-code-quality-report-reveals-growing)). A 2026 paper title says it plainly: "When 'Do Not' Is Not Deny" ([arXiv 2608.23550](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23550)). Destructive incidents follow the ambient-credential pattern ([OECD](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents/2026-04-27-6153)). A workspace with files, a browser and network access has all three legs of Willison's lethal trifecta by default ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/)).

**(b) Principle.** *If a rule matters, the workspace enforces it. If it can't be enforced, it is a suggestion and labelled as one.* Agents get scoped, per-task credentials and never ambient ones.

**(c) Why bold.** The ecosystem's answer to "the agent ignored my rule" has been longer instruction files, which evidence says can hurt ([arXiv 2602.11988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988v1)). Canopy would treat rules as policy code and shrink instruction files. The risk: agents feel slower and more constrained, and policy authoring becomes a new burden. Enforced policies must be few and high-stakes.

**(d) In the product.** A team rules editor where each rule is tagged *enforced* (compiled into hooks, permission scopes, path locks, protected tests, package allowlists, egress allowlists) or *guidance* (kept in a short instruction file). Irreversible operations (drop, delete volume, force-push, production deploy, money movement) always require out-of-band human confirmation, with backups held outside the agent's credential scope. Credentials go through Canopy's vault as a proxy, so generated code never sees them, following the Managed Agents pattern ([Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents)). Agents that read untrusted content (web, email, tickets from outsiders) run without secrets or outbound write access. That cuts the trifecta structurally.

**(e) Teams and non-engineers.** Admins set guardrails once for everyone, including non-engineers who cannot judge whether a command is dangerous. This is how a company lets a marketer run agents against real repos. Anthropic's marketer cut ad-variation time from 30 minutes to 30 seconds with no coding background ([Generation Digital](https://www.gend.co/blog/anthropic-claude-ad-creation-30-seconds)).

**(f) Build on vs new.** Seeds: the password vault that fills logins without exposing them to agents, shared-credential brokering, hooks on every CLI, gated deploy with typed phrase, secret scan, and Companion authority levels. New: a rules-to-gates compiler, egress control, credential proxying for all engines, irreversible-action classification, and trifecta-aware agent separation.

### 9. Memory is an audited, scoped, expiring ledger

**(a) Problem.** Memory poisoning succeeds at high rates and tested defenses fail ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-memghost-agent-memory-poisoning-20260723-c/)). Shared stores spread poison to other users' sessions ([Promptfoo](https://www.promptfoo.dev/lm-security-db/vuln/agent-persistent-memory-poisoning-7e5fb607)). Errors become sticky ("context poisoning") ([iThome](https://ithelp.ithome.com.tw/articles/10397270)). Pruning stale context and external memory improved results by 29–39% internally at Anthropic (vendor) ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/context-management)). Misalignment carries across sessions: 0.519 probability of a misaligned next session after a misaligned one, against 0.336 otherwise ([arXiv 2605.29442](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29442v1)).

**(b) Principle.** *Every memory has an author, a source, a scope and an expiry. Nothing derived from untrusted content becomes shared memory without human approval.*

**(c) Why bold.** Products sell "the agent remembers everything" as magic. This makes memory slower to accumulate and visible, which is less magical. It accepts that agents will sometimes lack a memory they "should" have had.

**(d) In the product.** Team memory is a ledger of typed entries (decision, constraint, convention, fact) with provenance links to the session or receipt that produced them. This is the event-sourced design PROJECTMEM proposes ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12329)). Writes from sessions that touched untrusted content land in quarantine. Entries are scoped (personal, project, team) and expire unless reconfirmed. The engine gets just-in-time retrieval from the ledger, not a growing preloaded file. A "stale or contradicted" detector flags entries that recent sessions contradicted.

**(e) Teams and non-engineers.** Decisions made in one person's session ("we chose Postgres row-level security, not app-level checks") become team knowledge with a reason attached. That partly replaces the hallway knowledge-sharing that agent use is eroding ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic)). Non-engineers can browse what the team has decided in plain language.

**(f) Build on vs new.** Seeds: project notes agents read and write, Companion memory, agent-memory settings, instruction-file management, research jobs with sources. New: provenance, quarantine, scoping, expiry and contradiction detection, plus making the ledger the source that instruction files are generated from, kept short.

### 10. The workspace measures spend and outcomes, so autonomy is earned and ROI is visible

**(a) Problem.** Developers misjudge their own productivity by about 40 points ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)). Organizations have no per-task cost attribution and spend sits on "one shared API key with no owner" ([Unblocked](https://getunblocked.com/blog/finops-for-ai-coding/)). 38% of organizations set usage limits ([Stack Overflow](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/10/06/the-results-of-the-2026-developer-survey-are-here/)). Pricing opacity triggered Cursor's public apology ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/07/cursor-apologizes-for-unclear-pricing-changes-that-upset-users/)). No credible public data exists on cost per merged change.

**(b) Principle.** *Every plan has a budget, every receipt has a cost, and the team sees outcomes (rework, reverts, escaped defects), not self-reports.*

**(c) Why bold.** Agent tools avoid showing users that their AI work may not be paying off, and Canopy would show exactly that. It accepts that some users and teams will discover agents are net-negative for certain task classes and use them less, which reduces engagement in the short term.

**(d) In the product.** A budget per plan and per person, with live plan usage spent by each Canopy feature on the connected brain. Background work routes to the brain's cheaper model for exploration and triage. A team outcomes panel shows cost per merged change, receipt pass rate, revert and rework rate by task class and engine, and time-in-review. This data feeds item 4's autonomy ledger. It runs local-first with no default telemetry to Canopy, in keeping with the current privacy stance.

**(e) Teams and non-engineers.** Leads get the evidence that perception surveys can't provide, and non-engineers get cost safety: a prototype session can't burn $500 overnight. Per-feature caps stop background intelligence from silently draining the user's weekly plan limit.

**(f) Build on vs new.** Seeds: plan-usage chip, token/cost tray (full for Claude Code, partial elsewhere), PRs dashboard, credit billing for cloud. New: cross-engine metering, per-plan budgets, routing, and outcome analytics tied to receipts and provenance.

## Stop building more surfaces; start owning the loop

**De-emphasize** features that make Canopy a broader IDE rather than a better harness. Stop adding peripheral built-ins (clipboard history, DB UI, more native viewers, Android expansion) until items 1–5 ship. They are table stakes at best, and the evidence never names them as a pain. Stop investing in Google-Docs-style plain-text co-editing beyond v1. The evidence says agentic multiplayer means shared plans, sessions and previews, not co-typing ([team_coordination.md](../research_notes/Agent%20native%20workspace%20rethink/team_coordination.md)), so redirect it into item 6. Stop chasing line-level AI-review parity (CodeRabbit-style findings) as a headline. Ungrounded LLM judges are noisy and over-correct, so fold the review loop under receipts, where grounded verification works better ([RepoAudit](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/guo25n.html)). Stop shipping UI that adds more parallel agents without better verification. Gas Town's thousands of dollars a month and HN's burnout reports mark that ceiling ([Maggie Appleton](https://maggieappleton.com/gastown); [HN 45489884](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45489884)). Stop encouraging auto-generated instruction files. And stop maintaining two product modes (item 7).

**The biggest risks** fall into five groups. *Brain dependence:* every feature now rides on CLIs Canopy does not control. With one CLI, a Claude Code release that changes flags or output format can break every feature at once. The mitigation is the dated runner verification Canopy already keeps, contract tests pinned per Claude Code version, and the single brain resolver so a second CLI can be added later. *Policy risk:* Anthropic's stance moved three times in four months. Driving official CLIs under the user's own login is the sanctioned route today, but if headless use is restricted, Claude-subscription users lose depth. Single-vendor dependence is accepted for now; adding a second brain is the hedge once the first is solid. *Friction risk:* plans, receipts and gates slow the first five minutes, which is exactly where non-engineers drop off ([The Atomic Builder](https://atomicbuilder.beehiiv.com/p/the-first-five-minutes-of-vibe-coding-are-broken)). Every gate must scale with blast radius. *Security risk:* an intelligent workspace with a browser, files, a vault and team memory is a high-value target, and memory and prompt-injection defenses are unproven. *Evidence risk:* most quantitative support for the agenda is vendor data or preprints. Earned autonomy, receipts' effect on review time, and awareness injection in real teams are all unvalidated, so Canopy should instrument them (item 10) and be willing to reverse.

**Sequencing.** First, in the next quarter, lay the foundation that everything else depends on: make `defaultAgent` the workspace's brain: a required, verified "Connect your brain" onboarding, with Claude Code as the only brain, one brain resolver that every intelligent feature uses, per-feature quota metering, instant rollback on every agent, Canopy registered as Claude Code's IDE, and the canonical session log in `canopy-serviced`. Ship proof receipts (item 2) at the same time, because they are the clearest differentiator, they work even for CLI engines via hooks, and they immediately improve every existing surface. Second, attack alignment and review: team-visible plans (3), the review rebuild (4) and automatic awareness injection (5), all built on Canopy's existing mesh and claims. Third, collapse Build and Engineer into role lenses (7) and harden governance (8). The non-engineer lens should not reach real repos until enforced gates exist. Fourth, durable handoff-ready sessions (6), the memory ledger (9) and outcome measurement driving earned autonomy (10). Each depends on the session log and receipts being mature enough to trust.

## Conclusion

The industry's first answer to agents was more agents: more parallelism, more autonomy, more PRs. The evidence shows that answer saturating against human attention and trust. The next advantage goes to whoever makes agent output *checkable* and agent work *coordinated*. That is a workspace problem, not a model problem, and labs selling models have weak incentives to solve it across vendors and roles. Canopy's distinctive position comes from three things: it is engine-agnostic, it already runs agent-to-agent mesh and claims, and it already serves engineers and non-engineers in one product. That position is more defensible than any feature it currently markets, but only if it stops being a host for other vendors' loops and becomes the layer that owns state, evidence and intent around them.

The founder's instinct, "connect a brain first, then the workspace comes alive", is the right onboarding. Its deeper implication is that Canopy's moat should be the *receipt*, not the brain: a verifiable record of what was intended, what was done, what proved it, and who decided. If that record becomes the thing teams pass between people, tools and Slack threads, Canopy is the system of record for agent work rather than one more place to type prompts. The weakest assumption in this agenda is that teams will accept a little friction for a lot of trust. Canopy should test it early, with receipts and visible plans, before committing to the rest.
