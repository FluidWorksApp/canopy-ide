Canopy 0.5.3 gives you your own remote-access link, moves terminal sharing under Team, makes the companion answer Slack messages even when it has stopped, brings each new release to cloud workspaces automatically, and offers to update a workspace instead of showing an error.

### Slack messages wake the companion
A message to your companion on Slack used to get "I couldn't reach Jarvis: The companion is not running in Canopy right now" whenever the companion wasn't already running. That happened while it was still starting, after its agent stopped, or after Canopy gave up retrying, even though Canopy itself was open. The person on Slack can't see the companion or press Retry, so now their message starts it again and is answered once it's ready. Waiting for the start takes up to a minute and a half.

When the companion really can't answer, the reply now says why: it is turned off in Canopy, this computer has no agent CLI to run it on, or it couldn't start, followed by the error.

### Workspaces get each new release
A new Canopy release now reaches cloud workspaces on its own. A workspace that starts or wakes from hibernation runs the latest workspace software, so features such as branch sync no longer wait on a manual update.

### Update workspace
When a running workspace is behind, Canopy now says so with **Update available**, or **Update needed** when something in this version depends on it, instead of "This workspace is running an older Canopy host". The workspace owner can choose **Update workspace**:
- It shows each step while it works.
- It restarts the terminals and agents in that workspace.
- It keeps your files and projects.
- If the new version doesn't start, the workspace goes back to the one it was running.

If other people are connected, Canopy says how many before you confirm. Members see "Ask the workspace owner to update it."

A workspace can use **Update workspace** after it has started once on 0.5.3. Until then, hibernate and resume it to bring it up to date.

### Your own remote-access link
When you're signed in to Canopy, **Settings → Remote access** is now one switch. Your agents are at your own address, `https://<you>.remote.canopyide.dev/remote`, which stays the same every time. Open it on your phone, sign in to Canopy, and you're in. There's no PIN to type and no Cloudflare, ngrok or Tailscale to set up. Only you can open it. The switch shows the link, a QR code to scan, and whether it's connected.

To reach Canopy on the same Wi-Fi without going through the internet, press **Show address and PIN** for the local address. If you're signed out, the previous tunnel options are still there.

If the link isn't live on Canopy's servers yet, Remote access says so and starts working once it is, with nothing to change on your side.

### Share a terminal from the terminal
Sharing a terminal with your team now starts where the terminal is: press **Share** in its toolbar, or right-click its tab and choose **Share with team…**. You can also start from **Team → Share a terminal** and pick a running terminal. Choose **Watch only** or **Pair**. Pair starts a clean shell everyone can type in, without your Git or agent sign-ins, and it ends after an hour. Then press **Share + copy link**. Everyone who can open the project in that workspace can see it.

**Team** now lists what's shared under **Live now**, with Watch or Join, Copy link and, for your own, Stop. Sharing has moved out of the Workspaces window. The old team relay form, with its host address and join code, is gone. Teams connect through your Canopy account.

### Dialogs fit the window
Long dialogs, such as a proof receipt, no longer run past the top and bottom of the window. The title and buttons stay in place and the content scrolls. Session documents work the same way. Their changed-file list now shows short paths, with the full path on hover. It also says how many of the session's edits are still in the code, for example "Still in the code: 3 of the 5 edits this session wrote".

### Reopened agent terminals come back as their agent
After Canopy restarted, an agent terminal under **Pick up where you left off** could show as a plain terminal. **Reopen** then started an empty shell with the conversation's title. Reopened agent terminals now come back as their agent, in the same conversation. A terminal that can't be resumed no longer carries the conversation's name.
